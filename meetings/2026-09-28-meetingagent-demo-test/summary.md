## Key Points
- Quantum computers use qubits which, via superposition and entanglement, can be dramatically faster than classical computers for certain mathematical operations such as factoring large numbers.
- Most internet encryption (RSA, elliptic curve cryptography) secures websites, VPNs, certificates, and digital signatures, and could be broken by a sufficiently powerful quantum computer running Shor's algorithm in hours or days.
- The 'harvest now, decrypt later' attack is a present-day risk: adversaries can steal and store encrypted data today to decrypt it once quantum computers become capable enough.
- Post-quantum cryptography (PQC) algorithms, designed to resist quantum attacks and run on classical hardware, exist now; NIST released its first official PQC standard in 2024.
- Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) uses light physics to share encryption keys, with any eavesdropping attempt being detectable.
- Quantum random number generators provide truly unpredictable randomness for encryption keys.
- Four recommended practical steps: (1) take inventory of encryption usage, (2) prioritize long-lived confidential data, (3) build crypto agility into systems, and (4) engage vendors on their post-quantum roadmaps.
