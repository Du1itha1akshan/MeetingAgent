## Key Points
- Quantum computers use qubits which, through superposition, can represent zero and one simultaneously, making them dramatically faster at certain mathematical problems like factoring large numbers.
- Most internet security relies on public key encryption (e.g., RSA, elliptic curve cryptography), which a sufficiently powerful quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could break in hours or days.
- The 'harvest now, decrypt later' attack is a present-day risk: adversaries can steal and store encrypted data now, then decrypt it once quantum computers are capable enough.
- Post-quantum cryptography offers encryption algorithms resistant to quantum attacks that run on current hardware; NIST released the first official standard in 2024.
- Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) uses light physics to detect eavesdropping on key exchanges.
- Quantum random number generators provide truly unpredictable randomness for encryption keys.
- Four recommended practical steps: take inventory of encryption usage, prioritize long-lived confidential data, build crypto agility into systems, and engage vendors about their post-quantum readiness.
